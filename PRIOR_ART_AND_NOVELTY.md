# Prior Art and Novelty Boundary

SFE sits near several established research areas:

- stochastic processes and stochastic dynamical systems;
- stochastic differential equations;
- Monte Carlo simulation;
- correlated and fractal random fields;
- Markov and non-Markov processes;
- dynamical systems and bifurcation theory;
- evolutionary computation;
- cellular automata and agent-based modeling;
- ensemble forecasting;
- constrained optimization;
- uncertainty quantification;
- procedural generation.

Terms such as *fractal evolution*, *stochastic fractal search*, and fractal/random-field evolutionary methods have prior uses. Therefore neither the name SFE nor the use of fractals plus randomness is evidence of novelty.

## Candidate contribution to test

The proposed contribution is the integrated cycle:

```text
multiscale seed
 -> state/history-dependent transition class
 -> seeded stochastic proposal
 -> explicit constraint operator
 -> recursive state update
 -> ensemble repetition
 -> attractor analysis
```

## Required novelty test

For every claimed SFE capability, identify the closest established baseline and ask:

1. Can the baseline express the same model?
2. Does SFE produce different measurable outcomes?
3. Are those differences useful?
4. Are they caused by a specific SFE component?
5. Do results survive sensitivity analysis and independent replication?

## Scientific language

Until such comparisons are complete, describe SFE as a **proposed experimental framework for constrained multiscale stochastic evolution**, not as an established new scientific field.
