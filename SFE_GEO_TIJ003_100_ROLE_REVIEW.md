# SFE-GEO-TIJ003 — 100-role Evidence Review: Pre-Sandia Tijeras River Hypothesis

**Status:** Literature-based research report. The 100 roles are an analytical work-breakdown, not 100 independent chatbot executions or votes.

## Central question
Did a large throughgoing river occupy the Tijeras structural corridor before the main uplift of the modern Sandia Mountains, possibly draining into a closed basin?

## Important evidence

1. Gorham & Ingersoll (1979) document Eocene Galisteo fluvial channels and floodplains, demonstrating regional river systems but not a river along the modern Tijeras Canyon route. DOI: https://doi.org/10.56577/FFC-30.219
2. Cather (1992) interprets the Tijeras fault as a releasing bend that controlled Eocene Galisteo half-graben subsidence. DOI: https://doi.org/10.56577/FFC-43.109
3. Galisteo stratigraphy and paleocurrents show fault-related depositional gradients and NE-directed paleoflow; these do not prove throughgoing west/east flow in Tijeras Canyon. https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/publications/periodicals/nmg/19/n4/nmg_v19_n4_p89.pdf
4. Sandia National Laboratories (2003) reports early Tertiary pull-apart conglomerates near Travertine Hills, but conglomerates may be local alluvial-fan deposits. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2172/913520
5. USGS Hedlund & Cordell (1985) describes the east-tilted Sandia fault block and principally Laramide Tijeras fault system. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3133/mf1631D
6. Kelley & Northrop, Memoir 29, documents structural control on the Tijeras canyon network. https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/publications/monographs/memoirs/29/home.cfm

## Evidence grades
- Ancient Tijeras structural weakness: supported.
- Regional Eocene fluvial systems: supported.
- Structural control on Galisteo basin sedimentation: supported.
- A large pre-Sandia river through the present canyon: unverified.
- Eastward flow into an ancestral Estancia endorheic basin: unverified.
- Continuous drainage through the Pecos–Estancia–Tijeras corridor: unverified.

Allocate ten distinct analytical tasks within each of ten groups:
1. Structural mapping and fault chronology.

3. Paleocurrents and channel geometry.
4. Detrital provenance and clast lithology.
5. Geochronology and thermochronology.
6. Paleotopographic reconstruction.
7. Endorheic basin history and hydrology.
8. Geophysical subsurface imaging.
9. SFE numerical hindcasting and competing baselines.

Every role must produce source-backed findings, uncertainty, and explicit disconfirming evidence. **No 100-agent execution or consensus has occurred.**

## Testable competing hypotheses
H1: older throughgoing river predated modern Sandia relief.
H2: fault-guided drainage arose during/after uplift.
H3: local Eocene streams fed fault-bounded Galisteo depocenters without crossing the modern canyon.
H4: drainage was reorganized or reversed by tectonic tilting.
